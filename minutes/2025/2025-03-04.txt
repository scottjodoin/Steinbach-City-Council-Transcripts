7. Council Meeting recessed and Public Hearing was called to order at 7:35 p.m., by Mayor
Funk. Public hearing was to consider the City of Steinbach 2025 Financial Plan.
7.1 Brian Hrehirchuk, Senior Manager, Finance, presented the 2025 Financial Plan.
Mr. Hrehirchuk provided information on the annual budget cycle and that City Council,
Management and staff have been working on the budget for several months, with Council providing comments at the final review in November.
7.2 Mr. Hrehirchuk presented the budget proposing a municipal property tax rate of 14.4 mills for 2025.
7.3 Mr. Hrehirchuk provided information on what to expect for 2025 Expenses Programs and
Projects and Debt that make up the 2025 budget:
- $9 million for new capital improvements:
- Events Centre
- $27.5 million capital renewal program for the replacement of equipment and

infrastructure:
- Loewen Boulevard and PTH 12 North Intersection
- Lift Station #1 Upgrade and Force Main Twinning
7.4 Mr. Hrehirchuk reviewed the municipal budget for revenues and expenses for 2025.
7.5 Mr. Hrehirchuk stated that there was strong development in 2024 and property value growth of 14.9%. Supplementary property taxes of $675,000.00, a $4.65 million provincial per capita tax sharing grant and Federal Gas Tax Grant of $1.03 million are expected.
7.6 Mr. Hrehirchuk provided a long-term overview for expected requirements in future years.
7.7 Mr. Hrehirchuk completed his presentation at 7:46 p.m.
7.8 Mr. Hrehirchuk was prepared to answer questions of council.
8. [Resident 1], 534 Wilson Street, was present at the public hearing and asked for clarification regarding the methods used to assess if the City’s infrastructure needs are being met.

--- Page 2 ---

9. City Manager Troy Warkentin explained that the City maintains a long-term capital plan extending beyond the required five-year outlook, spanning 7-10 years. The Engineering
Department utilizes mathematical models and industry benchmarks to assess infrastructure capacity, ensuring the system can sustain current demands while accommodating future growth.
With relatively young public infrastructure built from more resilient materials, the City benefits from longer asset lifespans compared to other urban jurisdictions. Additionally, various models are used to evaluate pavement conditions, with consultants providing analysis to guide maintenance and improvements.
10. There being no further discussion, the Public Hearing closed, and the Council

meeting re-opened at 8:00 p.m., and the following resolutions resulted.
R25-048 11. Councillor M. Zwaagstra, Councillor S. Penner
WHEREAS Section 162 of The Municipal Act, SM 1996, c.58 states:
“Every council must adopt a financial plan for each fiscal year in a form approved by the minister and consisting of (a) an operating budget; (b) a capital budget; (c) an estimate of operating revenue and expenditures for the following fiscal year; and (d) a five-year capital expenditure program.”
NOW THEREFORE BE IT RESOLVED that the financial plan for the City of Steinbach for the year 2025 as set out in the manner and form approved by the Minister be and the same is hereby approved in principle, the determination of school tax requirements, and that the said plan information shall be incorporated in and form part of the 2025 Tax Levy By-Law.
-Un. Carried-
12. Council meeting recessed and Public Hearing was called to order at 8:02 p.m., by
Mayor Earl Funk. Public Hearing was to consider Variance V-2025-02.
Variance V-2025-02
Owner: 268 Home Street - Kingdom Home Building Inc.
274 Home Street - [name withheld] and [name withheld]
Applicant: Kingdom Home Building Inc.
Civic Address: 268 & 274 Home Street
Legal: NW 82.5 Feet & SE 82.5 Feet of Lot 7, Block 3, Plan 3906
Purpose: To allow a maximum height of 4 stories for a multi-family building as the maximum height in the “RMD” Residential Medium Density Zone is 3 stories for the purpose of having grade level parking with 3 stories of residential above.
12.1 Troy Warkentin, City Manager introduced Variance V-2025-02 and reported that notices pursuant to The Planning Act had been completed.
12.2 One item of objection had been received from [name withheld], 251 Home Street, objecting to the application.
12.3 Luke Wiebe, 30017 36E Hirschfeld Road, of Kingdom Home Building Inc. was present at the hearing and presented the following:
- Previous public hearing materials from approximately one year ago depicted a partially underground parkade, with 6 to 6.5 feet below grade and the remainder above ground.
- The variance request seeks approval for a height increase to accommodate a heated parkade beneath three stories of residential units.
- The building’s design and style remain unchanged.
- Mr. Wiebe outlined the height differences between a main-floor parking structure and a partially underground parkade, stating he was open to either option, as both are viable plans.
- He requested council approval for the variance, with the final decision on parking configuration to be determined through the development agreement and engineered construction plan submission process.
- Mr. Wiebe was prepared to answer questions of council.
13. Council discussion and questions followed.

14. [Resident 2], 262 Home Street, was present at the public hearing to object. He read a letter from [name withheld] of 412 Hospital Street, who expressed concerns about the impact on neighboring homes and privacy, stating they believe the location is unsuitable.
They also noted that one of Steinbach’s remaining heritage trees may not survive the development.
[Resident 2] personally raised privacy concerns, stating he is uncomfortable with the prospect of residents overlooking his backyard. He also noted that due to the building’s height, his backyard would receive no sunlight until mid-afternoon. As a result, he is considering relocating rather than investing in improvements to his home.
15. [Resident 3], 243 Home Street, was present at the public hearing to object  e raised concerns about the lack of privacy for residents in their yards and homes, as well as the limited sunlight due to the building’s height. He expressed opposition to the removal of large, healthy trees that would be lost to development. Additionally, he noted safety concerns, stating he was not allowed to play in his front yard due to the speed of traffic.
16. [name withheld], 243 Home Street was present at the public hearing to state that their neighbourhood is the most inappropriate place to have a development like the one proposed.
They have been surrounded by construction with the event centre and the hospital.
17. [Resident 5], 251 Home Street, was present at the public hearing to object to the variance, expressing concerns about its impact on her family and the neighbourhood. She cited increased traffic, longer wait times at intersections, and a higher risk of accidents as major concerns. She raised objections to the noise levels associated with the parking structure, including revving engines, slamming car doors, and alarms. She expressed that the development would negatively affect the character and aesthetics of the neighborhood and highlighted potential structural risks to nearby buildings, noting that deep foundational work could cause soil shifting, increased drainage issues, and erosion. She also raised concerns that excavation could disturb the root systems of large, established trees in the area.
18. Mr. Wiebe returned to the podium to acknowledge the concerns raised, emphasizing his appreciation for downtown, having grown up near the event centre on Reimer Avenue. He shared that many of his renters choose to live downtown for its walkability and to support local businesses. While recognizing that change can be difficult, he assured those present that he is mindful of their concerns and will work closely with City engineers to ensure the development is built safely.
