CITY OF STEINBACH
Regular Council Meeting
September 1, 2026

MINUTES

1. Minutes of the Regular Council Meeting of City of Steinbach Council held on Tuesday,
September 1, 2026, at City of Steinbach Council Chambers.

2. Mayor Earl Funk called the meeting to order at 7:30 p.m., with the following members of
Council present: Councillors Susan Penner, Michael Zwaagstra, Jac Siemens, Bill Hiebert & Jake
Hiebert. Also present: City Manager, Troy Warkentin, Senior Manager, Corporate Services, Adam
Thiessen, and City Clerk, Amanda Dubois.

3. Councillor M. Zwaagstra opened the meeting.

R26-176 4. Councillor B. Hiebert, Councillor S. Penner RESOLVED that the agenda be adopted.

R26-177 5. Councillor J. Hiebert, Councillor J. Siemens RESOLVED that the minutes of August 18,
2026 Regular Council Meeting be approved.

-Un. Carried-

R26-178 6. Councillor J. Hiebert, Councillor B. Hiebert RESOLVED that Councillor Susan Penner be excused from the August 18, 2026 Regular Council Meeting and Councillor Damian Penner be excused from the September 1, 2026 Regular Council Meeting.

-Un. Carried-

7. Council meeting recessed and Public Hearing was called to order at 7:36 p.m., by
Mayor Earl Funk. Public Hearing was to consider Rezoning By-Law 2285.

By-Law 2285 - Rezoning

Owner: 3XL Holdings Ltd.
Applicant: Jordan Loewen

Civic Address: 58 Giesbrecht Street

Legal: Lot 2, Plan 14811

Purpose: To rezone the properties from “RLD” Residential Low Density to “RMD” Residential
Medium Density to allow for multi-family to be constructed.

7.1 Troy Warkentin, City Manager introduced By-Law 2285 and reported that notices pursuant to The Planning Act had been completed.

7.2 Troy Warkentin, City Manager provided that one item of correspondence had been received.

7.3 Troy Warkentin, City Manager provided that written correspondence has been received from Manitoba Municipal Relations - Community Planning Branch regarding By-Law 2285 voicing no concerns.

8. The applicant Jordan Loewen, 444 Hespeler Street South, representing 3XL Holdings
Ltd., was present at the public hearing and provided the following:

- The property was purchased this year, and is at the end of its life cycle. He believed the project would be a good fit for the area, being on a large lot, only building two stories with twelve units.
- There were 13 RMD lots of infill within a block of a half. His site plan meets all requirements, including setbacks, parking, garbage and recycling, landscaping and buffering. He will be obtaining a lot grading plan to address drainage.

9. [Resident 1], 17 Westview Place, was present at the hearing and explained that her property was directly affected. They had lived in their home since 2013, and the back has been an open field. She expressed concerns regarding privacy, the mature trees which exist, lights, traffic, noise, people cutting through their property, flooding and the potential for the resale value of their home to be reduced. She also shared concerns regarding the amount of traffic that uses Giesbrecht to enter Tim Hortons.

--- Page 2 ---

10. [Resident 2], 17 Westview Place, was present at the hearing with concerns with the amount of traffic added including the Lund Road project.

10.1 Councillor S. Penner requested clarification on the location of the project as it did not appear to directly line up with Westview Place.

10.2 [name withheld] asked if the parking lot would be at the back or front of the property.

10.3 Mayor Funk confirmed that the parking lot was shown at the front of the building and that all setbacks will be met.

10.4 [name withheld] questioned if the trees would be removed.

10.5 T. Warkentin, City Manager, clarified that there are certain requirements in the city zoning by-law regarding street edge landscaping. It is up to the developer to determine what the landscaping plan will entail.

11. [Resident 3], 30 Giesbrecht Street, was present at the hearing to express concern regarding water on the street and the water that will come off the paved lot. The drainage ditch beside him runs to PTH 52, and has improved greatly over the years, however, there is more water accumulating on the street. Giesbrecht Street is already a river in heavy rainstorms. He also had concerns regarding traffic as Giesbrecht is a main artery and urged council to open up another avenue.

12. [Resident 4], 316 McKenzie Avenue, was present at the hearing and believed that the city should address city planning instead of developer opportunities to earn extra income. He asked if the city had a plan instead of infill, that would be more appropriate and potentially less damaging for the immediate residents, which could also increase the amount of people you would like to bring in for tax increases and revenue.

He does not agree with the developer regarding the end of life of the home and likened it to a car, which will keep working until you stop fixing it. He felt it was inaccurate and opportunistic. Why replace one family home with twelve. That is a huge impact on residents. He would like the zoning in place to be respected and use future planning. Why not build a duplex? The people renting a twelve plex will potentially not take care of it.

He would like council to support homeowners who have been paying very good taxes. Would you enjoy or accept this in your backyard. He felt the developer should try to choose a lot available for this exact type of project. He also expressed concerns regarding tree replacement.

13. [name withheld], 54 Giesbrecht Street, was present at the hearing and explained they purchased the property next door to the proposed development a year ago. She raised concerns about privacy once the trees were cut down and asked if there were plans to put up a fence. One of the trees is on her yard but overhangs onto their property and had concerns regarding the survival of it. She asked about the building timeline.

14. Mr. Loewen returned to the podium to address concerns raised.

- The building will be setback 25 feet from the rear property line.
- As many trees as possible will be kept. There is a cost involved with cutting and hauling trees away, planting new trees and credit for existing trees that remain.
- The walkability factor makes this property ideal for twelve units. Home ownership can be difficult for some people. To be within walking or biking distance from companies such as
Loewen Windows and Resthaven is important to people.
- A fence will be installed around the property.
- Flooding concerns will be addressed with the lot drainage plan, which will be developed by a civil engineer.
- There is a by-law regarding lighting and parking lot lights will be shielded.
- The road is designated as a collector street and is designed to handle the additional traffic.
- He will do everything possible to keep the tree and just prune branches. He confirmed that he does the building and contracting and if they have concerns, they can call or text him. The earliest they would start the project would be June 2027.

15. There being no further discussion, Council meeting re-opened at 8:07 p.m.

Variance V-2026-17
