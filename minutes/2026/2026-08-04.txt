CITY OF STEINBACH
Regular Council Meeting
August 4, 2026

MINUTES

1. Minutes of the Regular Council Meeting of City of Steinbach Council held on Tuesday,
August 4, 2026, at City of Steinbach Council Chambers.

2. Mayor Earl Funk called the meeting to order at 7:30 p.m., with the following members of
Council present: Councillors Susan Penner, Michael Zwaagstra, Jac Siemens, Bill Hiebert, Jake
Hiebert & Damian Penner. Also present: City Manager, Troy Warkentin, Senior Manager,
Corporate Services, Adam Thiessen and City Clerk, Amanda Dubois.

3. Mayor Earl Funk opened the meeting.

R26-154 4. Councillor S. Penner, Councillor D. Penner RESOLVED that the agenda be adopted.

-Un. Carried-

R26-155 5. Councillor J. Hiebert, Councillor B. Hiebert RESOLVED that the minutes of July 21, 2026
Regular Council Meeting be approved.

-Un. Carried-

6. Council meeting recessed and Public Hearing was called to order at 7:35 p.m., by
Mayor Earl Funk. Public Hearing was to consider Rezoning By-Law 2283 and Subdivision
4451-2025-9083.

Rezoning By-Law 2283 & Subdivision 4451-2025-9083

Owner: William’s Creek Development Inc.

Civic Address: Meadow Grass Lane, William’s Creek Way, Golden Prairie Gate,
Kingston Way, Whispering Creek Cove & Sweet Water Lane
Legal: NLY 1520 feet perp of the NE ¼ of Section 36-6-6 EXC:
Firstly: Water Control Works plan 12770 and Secondly: Plans 16551 & 44292

Purpose of Rezoning By-Law 2283: To rezone proposed lots 1-153 to “RSU” Residential
Singe Unit, “RLD” Residential Low Density and “RMD” Residential Medium Density as part of a multi-phase residential development.

Purpose of Subdivision 4451-2025-9083: To create 153 residential lots as part of the first phase of a multi-phase residential development as well as the opening of Meadow Grass Lane,
William’s Creek Way, Golden Prairie Gate, Kingston Way, Whispering Creek Cove and Sweet
Water Lane.

6.1 Troy Warkentin, City Manager, introduced By-Law 2283 and Subdivision
4451-2025-9083 and reported that notices pursuant to The Planning Act had been completed.

6.2 Troy Warkentin, City Manager, provided that no items of correspondence had been received.

6.3 Written correspondence has been received from Manitoba Municipal Relations -
Community Planning Branch regarding By-Law 2283 and Subdivision 4451-2025-9083 voicing no concerns.

6.4. Marcel Jodoin, President, representing William’s Creek Development Inc. provided that the project had been in development for several years. He was prepared to answer questions of council.

7. Councillor J. Hiebert questioned if there were any commercial lots planned within the subdivision.

7.1 Mr. Jodoin confirmed that only single, duplex lots and multi-family were part of the development.

--- Page 2 ---

8. [name withheld], 86 Ashgrove Lane, was present at the hearing questioning how drainage would work, if there was going to be a fence installed along the walking path and what greenspace was being provided. Will all the duplexes be rentals and when is the estimated construction start date.

He also cited concerns that if low-income housing is in place it will bring down property values in the neighbourhood. Elaborating on drainage concerns, he explained he was dealing with drainage issues and has already flooded.

9. [Resident 2], 26 Ash Grove Lane, was present at the hearing and questioned what would appear behind his property. He understood that it would be a drainage ditch with a concrete down the middle. Would it eventually become a walking path, will there be a sidewalk in front of the houses and when would that be installed?

9.1 Mayor Funk explained that if sidewalks are to be installed, it is usually when development is 80% complete or more.

10. [Resident 3], 176 Wyndham Estate Drive, was present at the hearing and would like to see a detailed map that will show the complete plan of the developed area, including sidewalks, greenspace, proposed playgrounds etc.

He cited drainage concerns and would like to see a detailed plan of how the drainage has been dealt with. He has water in his backyard that has never been resolved. He invited council to come to his backyard and look as their water is running into his backyard because the city has not dealt with it.

In developments he had been involved with in other cities, designs had to be met to include sidewalks immediately by the developer. Trees were planted, with approved species given.
Playgrounds should be installed immediately so when people are moving in, they can enjoy them immediately.

He inquired about designated green space and what the green space will be used for. With 150 lots, is there a play structure? Where are the kids going to walk and play? He would also like to see planned bus stops like the Canada post community mailboxes, citing safety concerns.

[Resident 3] questioned the added density in relation to the amount of parking available and the city’s ability to clear snow in the winter. He requested clarity on where through traffic would be directed as well as construction traffic during the development stages and requested an immediate secondary exit to alleviate suggested traffic congestion.

[Resident 3] requested that the developer install a forever fence around the development and to restrict houses from having a gravel driveway, which does not appeal to housing values.

11. Mr. Jodoin returned to the podium and provided that the site concerns including parking will be in accordance of the site and parking requirements of the city of Steinbach zoning by-law.
Green space allocations and dedications will be addressed within the development agreement.

Bus stops are not part of the zoning by-law. Perhaps the city of Steinbach would consider it for future.

11.1 Mayor Funk confirmed that currently, the Hanover School Division situates their bus stops taking into consideration the number of children in the area and proximity to the school.

12. Mr. Jodoin explained that fencing requirements are outlined in the zoning by-law. There is no requirement for a fence as it is residential backing onto residential. He clarified that he intended to purchase lots 42-45 and personally build his home, which will be right along the drainage swale. The thirty-foot public reserve will require a concrete bottom, which will improve and address drainage issues.

Mr. Jodoin concluded by confirming that he has paid their engineers substantially to develop the drainage plan.

13. Councillor B. Hiebert inquired if William’s Creek Way will go all the way to Loewen
Boulevard?

13.1 Mr. Jodoin confirmed that construction vehicles will enter and exit through William’s
Creek Way.

--- Page 3 ---

14. [Resident 3] returned to the podium requesting the immediate plans for playgrounds and green space be addressed.

14.1 Mr. Jodoin returned to the podium and explained that green space would be addressed through the development agreement. Under the zoning by-law, there is a mandatory requirement for green space or a dedication fee of green space. The city has not dictated that a playground would be required. If a park would be required, it would be part of phase two on the east side of the Manning Canal, and would be a city requirement.

15. [Resident 1] returned to the podium to request that proposed lots 16-41 remain single family.
Technically, a duplex is like having two homes on one lot. Two driveways, two sets of vehicles.
Even with the lot being wide enough, 150 lots could equal 300 homes. Four homes, potentially eight, will be built behind his home and now the City doesn’t want to require a forever fence. He cited drainage and flooding issues in his area which cannot find the root of the problem or a solution to. He provided that he never had drainage issues until Wyndham was developed.

16. [Resident 3] returned to the podium to elaborate on drainage issues. What will the drainage look like from proposed lot 16 to lot 41. Where is that drainage going, especially when every lot can potentially be a duplex. He requested that the developer change proposed lot 16 to lot 42 be designated single family lots. This would help traffic flow and drainage.

17. Mr. Jodoin returned to the podium to reiterate that the drainage is developed by engineers, who work with the city engineer to meet all requirements. They approve, deny or request changes until it meets the city standards. If he wasn’t confident that the swale would address those concerns he would not build his own house on those lots.

18. [Resident 1] returned to the podium to request that proposed lots 16 to 42 remain as single family.

19. Mr. Jodoin returned to the podium and provided that the developer, subject to zoning, dictates what will go on a particular lot. One of the major considerations is demand and cost. A number of people cannot afford to purchase a lot and build a single-family home on it but can afford a duplex lot. In this development, they are trying to address those concerns and balance the needs of purchasers as they go through their eighth or ninth version of the development. Duplexes are quite appealing now compared to fifty years ago.

20. There being no further discussion Council meeting re-opened at 8:18 p.m.

27. Justus Oladele, Founder of KodaGo attended the meeting as a delegation. He provided the following information:
-
KodaGo is a ride share service created for Steinbach and operating throughout Southeast
Manitoba.
-
It currently has 246 users and serves Steinbach and other Southeast Manitoba communities with the goal of bringing rideshare and connecting communities across the southeast.
-
Passengers use an app to request a ride, track the driver in real time and receive an upfront fare estimate, whereas taxi fares are based on a meter and can vary.

28. Mr. Oladele requested that the City of Steinbach consider partnering and collaborating with KodaGo, focusing on medical services and airport.

29. Councillor S. Penner congratulated Mr. Oladele on his endeavor.

30. Mayor Funk suggested Mr. Oladele draft a letter to council with his request so they can consider his request further.
