CITY OF STEINBACH
Regular Council Meeting
October 06, 2026

MINUTES

1. Minutes of the Regular Council Meeting of City of Steinbach Council held on Tuesday,
October 6, 2026, at City of Steinbach Council Chambers.

2. Mayor Earl Funk called the meeting to order at 7:30 p.m., with the following members of
Council present: Councillors Susan Penner, Michael Zwaagstra, Damian Penner, Jac Siemens, Bill
Hiebert & Jake Hiebert. Also present: City Manager, Troy Warkentin, Director of Corporate
Services, Adam Thiessen, City Planner, Lacey Gaudet and City Clerk, Amanda Dubois.

3. Councillor S. Penner opened the meeting.

R26-197 4. Councillor D. Penner, Councillor B. Hiebert RESOLVED that the agenda be adopted as amended:

Item 9B - Board of Revision - Appointments - Postponed

-Un. Carried-

R26-198 5. Councillor J. Hiebert, Councillor J. Siemens RESOLVED that the minutes of
September 15, 2026 Regular Council Meeting be approved.

-Un. Carried-

6. Council meeting recessed and Public Hearing was called to order at 7:38 p.m., by
Mayor Earl Funk. Public Hearing was to consider Rezoning By-Law 2281.

By-Law 2281 - Rezoning

Owner: [name withheld]
Applicant: Owen Reimer

Civic Address: 330 McKenzie Avenue

Legal: Parcels A, E & ELY 47.5 Feet of Parcel D Plan 11979

Purpose: To rezone the subject property from “RLD” Residential Low Density to “RMD”
Residential Medium Density to allow for a multi-family unit building to be constructed on the property.

6.1 Troy Warkentin, City Manager introduced By-Law 2281 and reported that notices pursuant to The Planning Act had been completed.

6.2 Troy Warkentin, City Manager, advised that thirty-four items of correspondence had been received and included in the agenda package, with an additional twenty-eight items received after the package was prepared and provided to Council in a supplementary package.

7. The applicant Owen Reimer, 41 Cutlass Drive, representing the owners, [name withheld], was present at the public hearing and provided the following:

- The property is 100 feet wide and 300 feet long, making it just over 2/3 of an acre, and is currently a single-family home. It is boarded by the Free Church to the east, a large RLD lot and one residential property bordering on the west side.
- The proposed plan follows all setbacks, garbage and entrance requirements and no variances will be applied for.
- The proposal is to build a twenty-unit multi-family building, which will include 1, 2 and 3 bedroom units and ten garages on the east side with parking to be located on the west side, creating a 40-50 foot buffer between the proposed building and neighbours’ property. Decks and balconies would be located on the east side of the building.
- The required amount of parking for twenty units is thirty-three parking stalls with the current plan showing forty stalls.
- This style of building would allow for affordable senior and family living.
- A fence will be installed on the east, south and west sides of the property.
- This property would be ideal for infill development, intensification and rezoning based on the city’s policies and community plan.

--- Page 2 ---

7.2 Mr. Reimer was prepared to answer questions of Council.

8. Councillor M. Zwaagstra inquired if the applicant had spoken to the surrounding neighbours.

8.1 Mr. Reimer confirmed that he has spoken with the owners of the surrounding properties.
He provided that the church requested a fence be installed so no one parked on their property.
They also advised that they do their parking lot snow clearing in the middle of the night.

9. Councillor J. Hiebert asked for clarification on the location of the building.

9.1 Mr. Reimer explained that back of the building would face towards the back of the Free
Church parking lot. The parking lot would be between the building and the neighbouring residential property. Decks on the back side would be facing towards the church.

10. [Resident 1], 316 McKenzie Avenue, was present at the hearing to object. They have renovated and care for their home and property for the last twenty-five years. They are the direct neighbours to 330 McKenzie Avenue. They are being punished for owning a large yard in the city of Steinbach. The lure of a multiplex speaks louder then the lure of trees, community and family homes. She questioned if the by-law existed to protect its people and the zones they have chosen to live in and requested council deny the rezoning of 330 McKenzie Avenue.

11. [Resident 2], 1 Carillon Way, was present at the hearing to object and asked that council respect the neighbourhood and the people who have made decisions based on the city plan.
Rezoning will set a precedent and other proposals will follow.

12. [Resident 3], 408 First Street, was present at the hearing to object and stated the project would have an awkward and devastating impact on the surrounding homes. The property is not close to amenities. Residents are maintaining green spaces and tree canopies, which aid in drainage. This will also lower the value of quality homes within the neighbourhood. She questioned why this lot would be considered when there was a good quality home currently existing. She felt it was a waste and poor stewardship.

13. [Resident 4], 33 Cutlass Drive, was present at the hearing to object. He cited loss of trees, additional traffic and no sidewalk access. He questioned why zoning was in there in the first place. There is major development going on throughout Steinbach. Planning should eliminate the need for rezoning. People have lived there for twenty, thirty or forty years and are the backbone of their neighbourhood. He questioned what right people have to expect that their neighbourhood remain relatively the same. No one living in a single-family home wants to live next to an apartment building.

The developer will receive return on investment, the city will receive increased revenue, but what do the residents get besides more traffic and more people and the unlikelihood that they will see their property values increase. The building would be two stories, with no elevator and no sidewalk access, and he felt that wouldn’t be senior housing at all.

14. [Resident 5], 316 McKenzie Avenue, was present at the hearing to object. He was concerned where development should occur. Over 25 years, they have raised four children, invested enormous amounts of time, money and work into their property. Nearly 2000 grade five students have visited his property to enjoy and experience ice skating, cross-country skiing, outdoor movies, snowmobile rides, soccer games, volleyball, sledding, snowshoeing, quinzee building, tree climbing, scavenger hunts, bonfires, Bannock roasts and more.

If the proposed building were to go through the neighbours that plan to stay do not gain financially.
He suggested that they would live with the consequences. They would potentially lose the quiet, the mature trees, the privacy, the green space and the character that attracted families and others in the first place. There would be more vehicles, more activity and noise. 20 or more families would create a fundamental change in the character of the neighbourhood. He asked for thoughtful planning and to recognize that existing residents are stakeholders in Steinbach future.

15. [Resident 6], 325 McKenzie Avenue, was present at the hearing to object and agrees with everyone’s reasons for objecting. She explained that they had bought their property a couple of years ago, choosing their home for the neighbourhood, and urged council to consider the overall impact.

16. [Resident 7], 325 McKenzie Avenue, was present at the hearing to object. The neighbourhood is full of beautiful, mature houses and trees that have been there for a very long time. A multi-family complex does not fit their neighbourhood.

17. [Resident 8], 310 McKenzie Avenue, was present at the hearing to object. He purchased his home five years ago and two years later have been offered $200,000.00 more than what they purchased their home for and have not sold, as it is their home. That’s how it is supposed to be.
He asked that council not destroy that.

18. [Resident 9], 105 Coral Crescent, was present at the hearing to ask a fundamental question.
Does it have integrity? He urged council to do what they say they are going to do. If you vote to approve, you need to remove the word integrity off election placards.

19. [Resident 10], 407 Walnut Street, was present at the hearing to object. He agreed with what had been said to object to the rezoning.

20. [Resident 11], 195 Kroeker Avenue, was present at the hearing and stated he was the parent and grandparent of the [name withheld]’s. He felt that the property should remain for groups to enjoy in recreation and fellowship. Their backyard is enhanced by the mature trees on 330 McKenzie
Avenue and questioned if the neighbourhood would be enhanced as it is replaced by a concrete parking lot and will devalue surrounding properties.

21. [Resident 12], 107 Coral Crescent, was present at the hearing to object with concerns with the amount of cycling and foot traffic in the area, as well as a lot of kids who walk to the SSRS and middle school. He was concerned with the increase in traffic on a already busy street.

22. [Resident 13], 122 Granite Park Boulevard, was present at the hearing in support of the application. He confirmed that McKenzie Avenue has several multifamily developments in the area. He provided that the main floor walkup would accommodate seniors, more then the required number of parking stalls would be provided and that the project was only two stories, when it could be three stories.

23. [Resident 5], 316 McKenzie Avenue, returned to the podium to clarify that a precedent had been set for duplexes and fourplexes further down McKenzie Avenue, and that he felt there is no precedence for a building of this size.

24. There being no further discussion, Council meeting re-opened at 8:17 p.m.

R26-199 25. Councillor J. Hiebert, Councillor B. Hiebert RESOLVED that the City of Steinbach deny second reading to By-Law 2281, being a rezoning by-law.

-Un. Carried-

26. Council meeting recessed and Public Hearing was called to order at 8:19 p.m., by Mayor
Earl Funk. Public Hearing was to consider Rezoning By-Law 2286 and Variance V-2026-16.

Rezoning By-Law 2286 & Variance V-2026-16

Owner: Redbrook Holdings Ltd.

Applicant: Jordan Loewen

Civic Address: 288 Henry Street
Legal: Parcel 1: SLY 32.5 Feet of the NLY 129.5 Feet of Lot 1, Block 1, Plan 3906 EXC
WLY 212.5 Feet.

Parcel 2: SLY 32.5 Feet of the NLY 97 Feet of E½ of Lot 1, Block 1, Plan 3906

Purpose of Rezoning By-Law 2286: To rezone the subject property from “RLD”
Residential Low Density to “RMD” Residential Medium Density to allow a multi-unit building to be constructed on the property.

Purpose of Variance V-2026-16: To allow an overall site width of 65 feet whereas the
“RMD” Residential Medium Density Zone requires a minimum site width of 75 feet.

26.1 Troy Warkentin, City Manager, introduced By-Law 2286 and Variance V-2026-16 and reported that notices pursuant to The Planning Act had been completed.

26.2 Troy Warkentin, City Manager, provided that there was no written correspondence on file.

27. Gionathan Delvecchio, 522 Henry Street, on behalf of the applicant, Jordan Loewen, was present at the public hearing and provided the following:

-
Removing the old existing house and cleaning up the yard was a good start and this project will further revitalize the street.
-
The building would be 12 units, designed so the building is at the back of the lot with parking in the front. 20 parking stalls would be provided.
-
The property is in a great central location in relation to the hospital and Steinbach Bible
College.
-
There is a demand for housing in the area, and this building will fit nicely.

28. [Resident 14], 287 Henry Street, was present at the hearing, citing he has a good relationship with the owner but had concerns regarding the lack of privacy he already faces with the previous development. He has also lost sight lines to sunsets. He was concerned that large trees would be removed and a large parking lot would be the main view. Henry Street is a narrow street, made narrower in the winter months due to snowbanks. He felt the development would also take away on street parking spots. The street also deals with a lot of emergency traffic and ambulances. He felt that a six plex would fit better.

29. Mr. Delvecchio returned to the podium to clarify that due to some comments from Mr.
[Resident 14] with their previous development they did move the building back further into the lot for this plan.

30. Councillor M. Zwaagstra asked for clarification on the plan for the existing trees.

30.1 Mr. Delvecchio provided they would like as many of the existing trees in the back to remain as possible, with new trees being planted in the front.

31. [Resident 14], 287 Henry Street, returned to the podium to question how trees will be planted in the parking lot and requested council take that into consideration.
