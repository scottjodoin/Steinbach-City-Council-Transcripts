6. Council meeting recessed and Public Hearing was called to order at 7:37 p.m., by
Mayor Earl Funk. Public Hearing was to consider Variance V-2026-14.

Variance V-2026-14

Owners: Bridgepark Manor Inc.

Civic Address: 290 Stone Bridge Crossing
Legal: Block 2, Plan 32407 & Parcel A Plan 54264 excl all mines and minerals and other materials as set forth in the Crown Lands act.

Purpose of Variance V-2026-14:

1. To allow a maximum height of six stories whereas the “RMD” Residential Medium
Density Zone permits a maximum of three stories.
2. To allow for a parking ratio of one stall per unit whereas the “RMD” zone require a ratio of 1.5 stalls per unit.

6.1 Troy Warkentin, City Manager, introduced Variance V-2026-14, and reported that notices pursuant to The Planning Act had been completed.

6.2 Troy Warkentin, City Manager, provided that no items of correspondence had been received.

6.3 Councillor S. Penner requested clarification about the sanitary sewer and whether it would be drained through Georgetown.

6.4 T. Warkentin, City Manager stated sewer servicing for the subject property was not part of the Georgetown catchment area.

7. Nic Thiessen, 50 Granite Park Boulevard, representing Bridgepark Manor Group provided the following:

-
Bridgepark Manor was a not-for-profit group who had a waiting list for two plus years. They are seeking to add much needed senior housing.
-
The property is currently zoned RMD. Building only three stories is not viable. They would like to build six-storeys, approximately 81 suites, with heated ground level parking.
-
He felt that their design would be suitable for the area as it would be bordered by single detached and multi-family buildings, as well as the Steinbach Bible College dormitories. The building would be as far away from single family homes as possible.
-
Seniors typically generate less vehicular traffic. He expects an increase in foot traffic.

-
Drainage and sewer concerns will be addressed as they work through their stormwater retention and servicing plans. They will also be working on rerouting the existing drain on the property and adding a swale.
-
Their goal is to provide adequate parking. Existing buildings have shown a 1 to 1 ratio works.

8. [Resident 1], 270 Golfview Crescent, was present at the hearing and outlined the following concerns:
-
A proposed six-storey building would be incompatible with the surrounding three-storey neighbourhood. Concerns were raised about the lack of compatibility with the surrounding single and three-storey neighbourhood and the lack of information regarding the number of units and the potential impacts on, in her opinion, the already overtaxed sanitary sewer and drainage systems.
-
Additional residential density would increase pressure on existing infrastructure, expressed concerns regarding increased traffic, parking, pedestrian activity, privacy, neighbourhood character, property values, and the loss of existing green space that currently assists with land drainage.
-
In 2017, the property was rezoned from low density to medium density but residents who had objected at that time were unable to fully assess the impact because no development plan had been presented.
-
Concerns were also raised about the removal of green space and mature trees, increased stormwater runoff, additional demand on Lift Station No. 1, and whether planned sewer upgrades would adequately address current and future capacity requirements.
-
Several questions were asked regarding the proposed development, including the number of dwelling units and parking stalls, traffic impacts, drainage routes, environmental impacts, engineering studies completed, and the capacity of the existing sewer and drainage infrastructure to accommodate the development.
-
Referring to previous correspondence with City administration regarding drainage studies, she provided that no third-party study had been completed. She had previously requested that the
City commission an independent third-party assessment of Lift Station No. 1 and the sewer system, stating that previous assurances following multiple flooding events had not provided sufficient confidence to her that future flooding would be prevented.

9. [name withheld], 104 Albert Street, was present at the hearing and questioned if the approaches would be coming from Stonebridge or Albert Street. He also had concerns regarding sewer and water and would like to know why six storeys. He would like to see it remain as greenspace or three storeys.

10. [Resident 3], 251 Stone Bridge Crossing, was present at the hearing to object and with water concerns, traffic and parking concerns, especially at 8:00 a.m. and 4:00 p.m., school buses and parents are dropping off children. Having a six-storey building right in front of single-family homes with no transitional space other than a parking lot.

11. [Resident 4], 250 Stone Bridge Crossing, was present at the hearing with concerns regarding her house. It has flooded every year. For the past couple of years with sewage backup.
She wanted to know how it would be solved. Is the overflow water be drained down Millwork and will that affect the animal rescue. They dealt with engineers regarding the animal rescue and their plans failed the shelter.

12. [name withheld], 104 Albert Street, questioned the Fire Department's capacity to respond to emergencies involving six- or seven-storey buildings, particularly if multiple fires were to occur simultaneously and residents, including seniors, required evacuation. She stated they have lived on Albert Street for 37 years and had never experienced basement flooding until last year, when approximately one foot of water entered their basement. They also noted that when the three apartment buildings on Maplewood were constructed, they had been assured the back lane would be graded but stated that no work has been completed. They have taken steps to protect their property by installing a sump pump and backwater valves at a cost of approximately $7,000 and expressed that the city should also fulfill its responsibility by addressing the underlying infrastructure concerns.

2008, they have experienced multiple heavy rainfall events during which floodwater came within two feet of their home. During those events, they observed that nearby storm sewer drains were no longer accepting additional water, leading them to believe the stormwater system had reached, or

was close to reaching capacity, contributing to concerns about sewer backups. He noted that
Steinbach has experienced significant commercial and residential growth, much of which relies on

16. Mr. Thiessen returned to the podium to respond to questions and concerns.

16.1 He understood how frustrating it was with the flooding that was experienced. It was important to note that the city has recently upgraded the lift station capacities that serve Millwork
Drive where the proposed building’s sewer will flow into. Multiple factors will address sewer backups.

16.2 The development will drain to the ditch that runs along the active transportation corridor behind it. In the development requirements of the city, a project must hold the water back on site and only be discharged at the same rate as if it were a grass field. That ditch will fill up, which is the purpose of the ditch. When it fills up it means it is doing its job.

16.3 The city is working on other initiatives to bypass waters from other areas. Stopping development of projects like this aren’t going to move the needle on what’s happening in that ditch.

16.4 Mr. Thiessen does agree that when the buses are running and parents are dropping off or picking up their children it is congested.

16.5 The entrance and exit to the property will be off Stone Bridge Crossing.

16.6 There will be 81 suites. They are currently making some changes including attempting to get more indoor parking on the main floor. If they can build a couple more suites to help with the feasibility and affordability aspect they will as the cost per suite right now is quite high.

16.7 He had never experienced any vehicles running all night long at any of the seniors housing along Stone Bridge Crossing. He has lived in the neighborhood for thirteen years. As frustrating as it is from a resident standpoint, the cars will be there, regardless of the size or height of the building. The parking lot is inevitable.

16.8 There is land that was donated to the east. There are projects being proposed for that area, and those projects will access and contribute to the sewer drain beside Georgetown and Stone
Bridge.

Water and sewer concerns would be addressed with city engineers and outlined in a development agreement.

23. Mr. Thiessen returned to the podium and explained that they are not far enough into their design and need to have a site survey done to determine where those trees are in reference to the property lines. The goal would be to keep and save as many trees as possible and design the site to do so.

24. In response to comments from the gallery regarding property values, Mayor Funk stated that there was no evidence demonstrating that residential property values decrease as a result of adjacent multi-family developments.

25. Ms. [Resident 8] returned to the podium stated that she knew of two prominent families in her neighbourhood who had quickly sold their homes because they were anticipating that their homes will go down in value and they would not be able to sell them.

25.1 Mayor Funk stated that anticipated impacts on property values are speculative and do not confirm actual outcomes. He noted that he has spoken with multiple realtors and has not received confirmation that adjacent multi-family developments have resulted in decreased residential property values.

25.2 Ms. [Resident 8] retuned to the podium, stating that they are neighbours. Neighbours in a neighbourhood that take care of their homes and love Steinbach. She suggested that maybe they will start moving out because they are not being taken care of.

They have also lost the view of the sunset and will now lose the view of the sunrise. A six-storey building would devalue her house as she would lose all privacy.

29. Mayor Funk reminded everyone that the matter before council was to discuss a height variance and parking variance.

30. [Resident 3] returned to the podium and questioned whether the impact of increasing the building height from approximately 40 to 80 suites on the sewer system had been studied. He asked if the existing sewer infrastructure was designed to accommodate the additional wastewater

--- Page 5 ---

discharge associated with the proposed increase in density. [Resident 3] reiterated his opposition to the proposal and noted that a municipal election is upcoming.

31. Troy Warkentin, City Manager provided there was a development agreement in place that the previous owner was required to enter into as a condition of approval for the rezoning to residential medium density in 2017. Within the development agreement is a standard clause that the city requires of all developments, which in part states “…that the owner shall pay the cost of all utility services as may be required by the respective utilities to service the land such services to be installed to the satisfaction of the city engineer and respective utilities.” It also relates to the overall anticipated demand for the city’s infrastructure services, whether it be road, sewer, water etc.

31.1 Further… “that that the owner will be responsible to ensure that drainage in the immediate area of these lots functions properly and that a professionally prepared lot grading plan will be submitted and approved by the city prior to the issuance of the first building permit.” These are standard requirements upon development.

31.2 There are consultants who provide services for all developments within Steinbach. The results of the professional services are reviewed by the city and considered for its potential impact on the city’s infrastructure as noted by the city engineer.

31.3 Other than the items that were raised in his report that is included in council’s information package, the city engineer indicated he had no other concerns with the proposed development.

32. [Resident 1] returned to the podium to question if the consultants were independent consultants or if they were internal consultants? Were there external overseers of those reviews. She expressed concern that the findings of these reviews were sufficient to the city but may not be sufficient to the neighbours and requested more overseeing of developments.

32.1 Troy Warkentin, City Manager, clarified that the consultants would be under contract with the developer or landowner. The city’s various departments will work with the developer and their related consultants as development proceeds in accordance with the city’s requirements.

33. Ms. [Resident 8] returned to the podium to state that even though the developer may meet the requirements of the city, it seems like the requirements of the city are not sufficient to service the city. She asked how a developer would know what is necessary for the surrounding neighbourhood. They will only be concerned about their site, not how it will affect the rest of the city.

33.1 Troy Warkentin, City Manager, confirmed that established engineering standards are applied when reviewing developments. Stormwater drainage and sewer capacity are assessed using approved models and standard residential unit values to determine projected demand.
Developments are generally approved when the projected demand remains within the designed capacity of the overall system.

33.2 Site drainage standards are based on average storm events and historical weather patterns, balancing system performance with reasonable construction costs. It was noted that no drainage system can be designed to accommodate every extreme rainfall event, particularly those exceeding a one in one hundred year levels.

34. Mr. Thissen returned to the podium and advised that limiting the development to three storeys would significantly reduce the number of residential units. An existing drain crosses the property from Albert Street, restricting where construction can occur. As a result, the building footprint must concentrate on the upper portion of the site, as building over the drain is not permitted.
