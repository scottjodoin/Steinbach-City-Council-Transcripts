1. Minutes of the Regular Council Meeting of City of Steinbach Council held on Tuesday,
March 21, 2023 at City of Steinbach Council Chambers.

2. Mayor Earl Funk called the meeting to order at 7:30 p.m., with the following members of
Council present: Councillors Jac Siemens, Damian Penner, Jake Hiebert, Susan Penner, and
Michael Zwaagstra. Also present: City Manager, Troy Warkentin, Manager, Corporate Services,
Adam Thiessen, Senior Manager, Finance, Brian Hrehirchuk and City Clerk, Amanda Dubois.

7. Council Meeting recessed and Public Hearing was called to order at 7:40 p.m., by Mayor
Funk. Public hearing was to consider the City of Steinbach 2023 Financial Plan.

7.1 2023 Financial Plan

7.2 Brian Hrehirchuk, Senior Manager, Finance, presented the 2023 Financial Plan.

Mr. Hrehirchuk provided information on the annual budget cycle and that City Council,

Management and staff have been working on the budget for several months, with Council

providing comments at the final review in November.

7.3 Mr. Hrehirchuk presented the budget proposing a municipal property tax rate of 13.7

mills for 2023.

7.4 Mr. Hrehirchuk provided information on what to expect for 2023 Expenses Programs and

Projects and Debt that make up the 2023 budget:

- $43.5 million for new capital improvements:

- Events Centre Construction

- $23.8 million capital renewal program for the replacement of equipment and

infrastructure:

- Loewen Boulevard and PTH 12 North

- Lift Station #2 upgrade

- Park Road West upgrade

7.5 Mr. Hrehirchuk provided that the City of Steinbach is in receipt of the Notice of

Tax requirements for the Hanover School Division in accordance with Section 187 of

The Public Schools Act, under Section 188 for 2023 the amount to be raised by tax levy

is $13,597,855.00.

7.6 Mr. Hrehirchuk provided that the City of Steinbach is in receipt of the Notice of

Tax requirements for the Manitoba Education and Training - 202\3 Education Support

Levy in accordance with sections 182 and 183 of The Public Schools Act, 2024

Other Property to be raised by tax levy is $3,096,420.00.

7.7 Mr. Hrehirchuk, provided that the projected 2023 Municipal property tax mill rate would have a decrease of 2.8% being 13.7 mills. There is a 4% increase to utility rates, pending

approval by the Public Utilities board, a 2.7% increase for residential garbage collection rates, and an overall 2.5% fee increase for land use applications and permits. The combined residential mill rate will be 25.686, combined commercial mill rate will be 33.826 for 2023.

7.8 Mr. Hrehirchuk provided a long term overview for expected requirements in future years.

7.9 Mr. Hrehirchuk completed his presentation at 7:50 p.m.

7.10 Mr. Hrehirchuk was prepared to answer questions of council.

7.11 There was no one present with questions or concerns at the meeting.

7.12 Mayor Funk thanked Mr. Hrehirchuk for a well prepared presentation.

7.13 There being no further discussion the Public Hearing closed and the Council

meeting opened 7:51 p.m., and the following resolutions resulted.

R23-053 8. Councillor M. Zwaagstra, Councillor J. Hiebert
WHEREAS Section 162 of The Municipal Act, SM 1996, c.58 states:
“Every council must adopt a financial plan for each fiscal year in a form approved by the minister and consisting of (a) an operating budget; (b) a capital budget; (c) an estimate of operating revenue and expenditures for the following fiscal year; and (d) a five year capital expenditure program.”
NOW THEREFORE BE IT RESOLVED that the financial plan for the City of Steinbach for the year 2023 as set out in the manner and form approved by the Minister be and the same is hereby adopted, and that the said plan information shall be incorporated in and form part of the
2023 Tax Levy By-Law.

-Un. Carried-

11. Troy Warkentin, City Manager, reported that the City has received The 2022 Steinbach
Annual Traffic Count Program, as submitted by WSP Canada Inc. The traffic count and analysis program were conducted at 21 intersections in Steinbach. The traffic counts were conducted on
Thursday, October 20, Friday October 21, Thursday October 27 and Friday, October 28 during the weekday peak period from 3:00 p.m. to 6:00 p.m. The study also includes discussion on the signal warrants analysis and pedestrian control warrants at the unsignalized intersections.

11.1 Mr. Warkentin Highlighted the following sections:

1) Traffic volume trends

2) Traffic signal warrant analysis

3) Recommendations

- Recommendations for Unsignalized Intersections

- Signals are warranted at the intersection of PTH 52 West and Giesbrecht
Street. Signalization would improve the critical movement (northbound) LOS from F to B in the weekday p.m. peak hour.

- Recommendations for Signalized Intersections

- Signal timing changes could provide some improvement LOS in the weekday p.m. peak hour at the intersection of PTH 12 Nort hand Park Road West/East, with overall delay decreasing by 14 seconds. The critical through movement LOS would improve from F to D, reducing delay by 52 seconds, and the critical turning and cross-street movement
LOS would improve from F to E.
