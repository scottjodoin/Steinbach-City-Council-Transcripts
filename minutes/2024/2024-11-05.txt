6. Council meeting recessed and Public Hearing was called to order at 7:37 p.m., by
Mayor Earl Funk. Public Hearing was to consider rezoning By-law 2253.

By-Law 2253 - Rezoning

Owner: 5563985 MB Ltd. & 6135367 MB Inc.

Applicant: Kingdom Home Building Inc.

Civic Address: 40 First Street
Legal: Lot 1, Plan 5054

Purpose: To rezone the property from “RLD” Residential Low Density to “RMD”
Residential Medium Density to allow for a multi-family development to be constructed.

6.1 Troy Warkentin, City Manager, introduced rezoning By-Law 2253, reported that notices pursuant to The Planning Act had been completed.

6.2 Written correspondence has been received from Manitoba Municipal Relations -
Community Planning Branch regarding By-Law 2243 voicing no concerns.

6.2 Luke Wiebe, 30017 Road 36E, of Kingdom Home Building Inc. was present at the hearing and provided the following:
• This location is walkable to all downtown services and jobs, as well as two parks behind and across the street.
• Access to the building will be off Giesbrecht Street, with covered parking, lobby and elevator on the main floor with residents having the luxury and privacy of entry to their units on the second and third floor.
• They intend to build using high quality counter tops, feature walls, flooring and lighting.
• The current home on the property is at the end of its life cycle and will be removed.
• He has spoken with the owner of the apartment building next to the property.

6.3 Mr. Wiebe was prepared to answer questions of council.

6.4 [name withheld], 22 Wyndham Estate Drive, owner of the property of 44 First Street, was present at the pubic hearing. She agreed that the plan looks much better then what is currently on the property but would like to know if what they see today on the drawing will be what is actually built. She asked when the existing house would be demolished and also requested clarification regarding green space requirements and if a variance would be required.

--- Page 2 ---

6.5 Administration clarified that the lot is 72 feet wide. RMD properties require a minimum of 75 feet. A minor variance would be required, which could be approved by administration.

6.6 [name withheld], 30 Giesbrecht Street was present at the hearing with concerns regarding traffic, and how it will be controlled as the street already experiences high volumes in the morning and evening. He also cited existing drainage issues and asked what would be done to manage water.

6.7. Mr. Wiebe returned to the podium and confirmed that the design presented is the building he intends to build. He confirmed that the construction design is not complete, so slight changes in dimensions, balconies etc. could change. He explained that drainage could actually improve as the property is developed and with the aid of the flat roof. He confirmed that if the rezoning is approved, he will be demolishing the house. Mr. Wiebe explained that there is green space along the west side and will be adding trees as well as saving as many trees as possible.

7. There being no further discussion, Council meeting re-opened at 8.02 p.m.

R24-207 8. Councillor S. Penner, Councillor M. Zwaagstra RESOLVED that the City of Steinbach give second reading to rezoning By-Law 2253, subject to the execution of a Development
Agreement.

11. Troy Warkentin, City Manager presented an update to the rain event which occurred over southeastern Manitoba on September 16-17, 2024 and its impact in Steinbach:

11.1 Steinbach experienced approximately 6 inches of rainfall over two days, with more than 3 inches falling during the morning of September 17th. During and after the storm, the City's storm drainage and sanitary sewer systems were operating at full capacity.

11.2 Several dozen property owners in Steinbach contacted the City directly to report basement flooding caused by the storm.

11.3 The most intense rainfall occurred on a Tuesday morning, during regular working hours.
The City’s employees were generally available and were deployed as needed to assist with various storm response and mitigation efforts both during and after the event.

11.4 Since the storm, the City has been in communication with the Manitoba Emergency
Measures Organization (EMO) regarding the provincial Disaster Financial Assistance program and has shared information with the public about the EMO’s storm damage recovery program, which may be available to affected property owners.
