September 17, 2024, at City of Steinbach Council Chambers.

7. Council meeting recessed, and Public Hearing was called to order at 7:38 p.m., by
Mayor Earl Funk. Public Hearing was to consider Variance V-2024-12.

Variance V-2024-12

Owner: 6135367 Manitoba Inc.

Applicant: 6135367 Manitoba Inc.

Civic Address: 322 Reimer Avenue

Legal: Lot 16, Block 6, Plan 8532

Purpose of Variance V-2024-12: To allow a detached accessory building to have a total square footage of 1,232 square feet whereas single family dwellings in the ‘C2’ Commercial
Community Zone can have a maximum square footage of 700 square feet; and to allow the maximum height of an accessory building to be 29.5 feet whereas the maximum height allowance is 15 feet. The applicant is proposing to construct a detached garage on the rear side of the property.

7.1 Troy Warkentin, City Manager, introduced Variance V-2024-12, and reported that notices pursuant to The Planning Act had been completed.

7.2 The applicant was present at the public hearing and explained why the variance was required and was prepared to answer questions of Council.

7.3 There was no one present at the public hearing with questions or concerns

7.4 There being no further discussion Council meeting re-opened at 7:46 p.m.

R24-180 8. Councillor J. Siemens, Councillor D. Penner RESOLVED that the City of Steinbach approve Variance V-2024-12 subject to an approved site plan including a lot grading plan being submitted and approved by the City.

-Un. Carried-

11. Troy Warkentin, City Manager, reported on the significant storm that occurred over the last 24 hours. According to media reports, a total of approximately six inches of rain fell in
Steinbach during this period, and due to the saturated ground conditions and the amount of rain that fell, especially during this morning, the City experienced significant overland stormwater flows in all neighborhoods. He further provided that the City’s open drainage channels, creeks, and ditches throughout the City are either at capacity or over capacity, with some spilling over their banks. The stormwater flood pump at the Park Road West lift station was operating at 9 a.m. and continues to be in operation until the sewer system flows return to normal ranges. All lift stations are functional and pumping at full capacity. Overland storm water flows impacted many roads. Many peripheral gravel roads were impacted and 16 wash out areas were identified.
TG Smith Centre experienced overland flooding with water entering the building which resulted in damage to the ice surface. It is expected to be restored in 2 weeks. Staff are working to monitor & maintain City facilities and will do a full assessment of the impact on City facilities & infrastructure over the next several days. Furthermore, they will determine the number of residential or private properties affected by either overland flooding or sewer backups.

18. Councillor M. Zwaagstra discussed concerns regarding a homeless encampment on Main
Street addressing safety concerns for people in the area, businesses and people in the encampment. The question was asked of the City Manager for any information that has come to the city regarding this. He further asked council if they should look at creating a by-law or policy to deal with these concerns.

18.1 City Manager, Troy Warkentin, replied that over several years, these encampments have been dealt with on a case-by-case basis. City staff work with several groups (property owners,
RCMP & not for profit organizations) to deal with these cases. For the encampment at 447 Main
Street, the camp was first noticed sometime in August. It is the City’s understanding that a formal trespass notice was issued by the property owners to the unauthorized individuals as of
September 5th and as of September 16th, all individuals had left the property.

18.2 Further discussion followed between council resulting in council asking City administration to investigate how other municipalities/cities are dealing with homelessness/encampments and report back to council.
