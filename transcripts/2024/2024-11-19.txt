[00:00:11] Michael Zwaagstra (Councillor): Thank you, Mr. Mayor. Municipal government is often described as the level of government closest to the people. What this means is that municipalities are responsible for things that impact people directly. It may not sound exciting to talk about garbage collection, road repairs, underground infrastructure renewal, or lift station upgrades, but residents would be quick to notice if anything went wrong in any of these areas. For example, earlier this year, the City of Calgary faced an emergency situation when a major water main break led to serious water shortages in that city. Calgary residents were forbidden from watering their lawns or gardens, and were strongly encouraged to use as little water as possible when taking baths or washing their dishes. At first, it looked like this issue would be resolved in a week or two. However, the problem turned out to be much more serious than that. It took several months of repairs before water restrictions were finally lifted. Needless to say, it was a long summer for Calgary residents. The crisis in Calgary was a good reminder of the need to put first things first. Providing safe drinking water to your residents is not a luxury, it's an essential. People won't care how many climate emergency resolutions you pass, how many proclamations you announce, or how many flags you fly at City Hall if they can't turn on their taps and know that clean water is going to come out. One of the things that I appreciate most about serving on Steinbach City Council is that our council and staff have always remained focused on the essentials. The millions that we spend every year renewing our underground infrastructure and fixing our streets is proof that we place a high priority on providing good quality service to our residents. Spending money now on infrastructure upgrades is much more cost-effective than delaying repairs until we are in a crisis. Our asphalt overlay program, where we resurface several streets every year or so, long before they fall into serious disrepair, is a prime example of this approach. Obviously, Steinbach's streets are not in perfect shape, but I'm happy to compare them to the streets of any other city any day. Our city also prioritizes basic services, such as cleaning our streets and sidewalks after each major snowfall. This is only possible because of the outstanding work ethic of the staff in our Public Works Department. While many other cities appear content to let snow pile up to the point of making streets impassable, our staff ensure that streets are cleared so that Steinbach residents can safely drive their cars throughout the winter. This doesn't mean that our city focuses only on basic infrastructure. The fact that our $69 million Southeast Event Centre is nearing completion shows that it is possible to provide residents with top-quality recreational facilities while at the same time ensuring that our streets are fixed and tap water is flowing. In fact, I would suggest that the only reason we could do this event centre project at all is because of the fiscally responsible decisions made by successive councils over the last few decades. Our city's 5-year debt policy, where we pay off all loans in 5 years, has given us enormous flexibility in our planning process. Even the Southeast Event Centre loan will be paid off in 5 years, enabling us to move on to other projects. Since municipal government is closest to the people, we must focus on the things that matter most. While other cities might get distracted by hot-button political issues, Steinbach will always put first things first. Thank you.
